Based on the information provided, there appears to be a potential violation of the Regulation Best Interest (Reg BI) Care Obligation under section (2)(ii)(A) regarding the private placement offering by Issuer 3.

The transactions show that the broker recommended a significant investment in "Private placement offering by Issuer 3 (pre-IPO shares of Company B)" with three separate purchases totaling $325,000 between September and November 2023. This represents approximately 50% of the customer's total investment portfolio (which is valued at $650,000).

The potential violations under section (2)(ii)(A) relate to insufficient due diligence:

1. There is no evidence that the broker conducted reasonable diligence to verify that Issuer 3 actually possessed or had rights to the pre-IPO shares of Company B they were purportedly selling. This directly parallels the example in the SEC guidance where FINRA found violations when firms "failed to take reasonable steps to confirm that the Issuer of a recommended investment actually possessed rights to the shares."

2. The broker appears to have concentrated a very significant portion of the customer's portfolio (approximately 50%) in a single high-risk private placement offering without demonstrating adequate due diligence on the issuer, the legitimacy of the offering, or potential markups being applied.

3. The pattern of three separate purchases of the same private placement offering within a short timeframe (September to November 2023) suggests the broker may have continued recommending the same high-risk investment without conducting ongoing due diligence about the status of the offering.

While the customer does have an "Aggressive" risk tolerance and "Wealth Growth" as a financial goal, which could potentially justify some high-risk investments, the concentration level and apparent lack of due diligence regarding the private placement still represent potential violations of the Care Obligation under Reg BI section (2)(ii)(A).

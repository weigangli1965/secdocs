# Analysis of Potential Violations of Section 240.15l-1(a)(2)(ii) - Care Obligation

Based on the information provided, there appear to be significant violations of the Care Obligation under section (2)(ii) of Regulation Best Interest, specifically regarding the recommendation of investment products for this 89-year-old retired customer.

## Violations Identified:

### 1. Inappropriate Risk Level Relative to Customer Profile (§ 240.15l-1(a)(2)(ii)(B))

The customer's investment profile shows:
- Advanced age (89 years old)
- Retired status
- Limited income (45K per year)
- Limited portfolio value (350K) relative to net worth (850K)

Despite these factors which would typically suggest a need for capital preservation and income, the portfolio shows:
- 70% allocation to "High Risk" investments
- An additional 15% in "Moderate to High" risk investments
- Only 7% in low to moderate risk investments (Corporate Bonds and Treasury Bonds)

The allocation includes particularly concerning investments for an 89-year-old retiree:
- 15% in Cryptocurrency ETF (High Risk)
- 10% in Private Equity Fund (Very High Risk)
- 20% in Hedge Fund (High Risk)

FINRA guidance specifically notes violations when there is "recommendation of sufficient high-risk investments to customers with a moderate risk tolerance that the amount of high risk investments in the customer's portfolio becomes disproportionate (as low as 20% in some cases)." Here, the high-risk allocation is 70%, far exceeding this threshold.

### 2. Failure to Consider Customer's Investment Time Horizon (§ 240.15l-1(a)(2)(ii)(B))

At 89 years old, the customer has a significantly limited investment time horizon, yet the portfolio includes:
- Private Equity Fund (typically requiring 7-10 year lockups)
- Hedge Fund investments (often with lockup periods)

These investments are generally unsuitable for someone of advanced age due to their illiquidity and long investment horizons.

### 3. Excessive Risk Concentration (§ 240.15l-1(a)(2)(ii)(B))

While the customer profile indicates an "Aggressive" risk tolerance, the broker still has an obligation to ensure recommendations are appropriate given the customer's overall investment profile. The concentration of 70% of assets in high-risk investments for an 89-year-old retiree appears to place the broker's interests (likely higher commissions on these products) ahead of the customer's interests.

### 4. Speculative Investment Purpose Unsuitable for Age (§ 240.15l-1(a)(2)(ii)(B))

All transactions list "speculation" as the investment purpose, which is generally inappropriate as a primary strategy for a person of advanced age with retirement needs. The broker appears to have failed to consider the customer's age, retirement status, and appropriate investment strategies for this life stage.

## Conclusion

The broker appears to have violated the Care Obligation under § 240.15l-1(a)(2)(ii)(B) by recommending a series of investments that, while potentially aligning with the stated "Aggressive" risk tolerance, fail to consider the customer's age, retirement status, likely need for liquidity, and limited investment time horizon. The disproportionate allocation to high-risk investments (70%) for an 89-year-old retiree suggests the broker did not have a reasonable basis to believe these recommendations were in the best interest of this particular retail customer based on their complete investment profile.

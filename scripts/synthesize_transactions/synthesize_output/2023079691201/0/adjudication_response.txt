# Analysis of Potential Regulation Best Interest Violations under Section (2)(ii)

After reviewing the customer profile, portfolio allocation, transaction history, and the relevant law, I believe there are violations of the Care Obligation under section (2)(ii) of Regulation Best Interest. Here are the specific violations:

## Violation 1: Disproportionate High-Risk Investments

The broker recommended high-risk investments that were inappropriate for this customer's risk tolerance. Specifically:

- The customer has a "Moderate" risk tolerance
- The portfolio contains 50% in high or very high-risk investments: 40% in REITs and 5% in Technology Sector ETF, 5% in Growth Stock Fund (moderate to high)
- The customer has limited investment experience (3 years)
- The customer's High Risk Investment Ratio is 60% and HighRiskNetworthRatio is 45%

FINRA guidance specifically notes violations occur when recommending "sufficient high-risk investments to customers with a moderate risk tolerance that the amount of high risk investments in the customer's portfolio becomes disproportionate (as low as 20% in some cases)."

## Violation 2: Inappropriate Recommendation of Non-Traded REIT

The first and largest transaction (May 15, 2021) was for a non-traded REIT accounting for 40% of the portfolio. This appears problematic because:

- Non-traded REITs are high-risk, illiquid investments that typically require significant due diligence
- The customer has limited investment experience (3 years)
- The concentration (40% of portfolio) in a single high-risk product is excessive for a customer with moderate risk tolerance
- The customer's financial goal is "Retirement," which typically requires a more balanced approach rather than heavy concentration in illiquid investments

## Violation 3: High Fees and Transaction Costs

The transactions show unusually high fees:
- The non-traded REIT transaction incurred $2,000 in fees and $800 in other transaction costs on a $40,000 investment (7% total cost)
- The S&P 500 Index Fund transaction shows $500 in fees and $100 in other costs on a $10,000 investment (6% total cost)
- Total fees and transaction costs across all transactions are $3,500 + $1,200 = $4,700 on a $100,000 portfolio

These fees appear excessive and the broker failed to consider reasonably available alternative investments at lower cost, as indicated by FINRA guidance.

## Conclusion

The broker violated the Care Obligation under section (2)(ii) of Regulation Best Interest by:
1. Recommending a disproportionate amount of high-risk investments
2. Concentrating 40% of the portfolio in a single high-risk, non-traded REIT
3. Charging excessive fees without considering lower-cost alternatives

These recommendations do not appear to be in the best interest of this particular retail customer based on their investment profile, especially considering their moderate risk tolerance, limited investment experience, and retirement financial goals.

# Violations of Section 240.15l-1(a)(2)(ii) Care Obligation

Based on the information provided, there are several violations of the Care Obligation under section (2)(ii) of Regulation Best Interest:

## 1. Trading During Market Halts

The most significant violation relates to all transactions being conducted "during trading halt." Trading during market halts raises serious concerns under the Care Obligation for several reasons:

- The broker has failed to exercise reasonable diligence, care, and skill to understand the potential risks associated with trading securities during halts
- Trading halts are typically imposed due to pending material news or regulatory concerns, and trading during these periods violates market rules
- The broker failed to conduct sufficient due diligence to ensure these transactions were permissible and in the customer's best interest

This pattern demonstrates a failure to understand the nature and features of the transactions before recommending them, which is explicitly noted as a violation in the SEC guidance.

## 2. Risk Profile Mismatch

The customer has a "Moderate" risk tolerance, but:

- 35% of the portfolio is allocated to high-risk investments
- Multiple high-risk investments (Technology Sector ETF, REIT securities, Cryptocurrency ETF, and Hedge Fund shares) were recommended
- The investment in high-risk products represents a disproportionate amount of the customer's portfolio relative to their moderate risk tolerance

FINRA specifically notes violations when high-risk investments become disproportionate for customers with moderate risk tolerance, mentioning that as low as 20% can be considered excessive. At 35%, this portfolio clearly exceeds that threshold.

## 3. Due Diligence Failures

The transactions show patterns that suggest insufficient due diligence:

- Recommending trading during halts indicates the broker failed to understand the regulatory restrictions
- The broker appears to have failed to investigate the legality and suitability of trading halted securities
- No evidence is provided that the broker verified the risks associated with these transactions

## 4. Potential Excessive Trading Concerns

While the data shows an annualized portfolio turnover of 5.8 and a cost-to-equity ratio of 18.5%, these metrics approach concerning levels:

- The turnover rate of 5.8 is very close to 6, which FINRA identifies as generally indicating excessive trading
- The cost-to-equity ratio of 18.5% is approaching the 20% threshold that FINRA considers problematic

These metrics, combined with the pattern of frequent trading during a short period (7 transactions in less than 3 months), suggest the broker may be engaging in a series of transactions that, when viewed together, could be considered excessive.

## Summary

The broker has violated the Care Obligation under section (2)(ii) by:
1. Recommending trading during market halts without proper diligence
2. Recommending high-risk investments that exceed appropriate allocation for a customer with moderate risk tolerance
3. Failing to conduct sufficient due diligence on the transactions
4. Approaching levels of trading activity that raise concerns about excessive trading

These violations demonstrate a failure to act in the customer's best interest and place the customer's investment profile ahead of other considerations.

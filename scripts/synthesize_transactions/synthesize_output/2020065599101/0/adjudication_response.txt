# Analysis of Violations Under Section 2(ii) of Regulation Best Interest

Based on the provided customer information, portfolio allocation, transaction history, and regulatory guidance, there are significant violations of the Care Obligation under Section 2(ii) of Regulation Best Interest. These violations primarily concern the mismatch between the customer's risk profile and the recommended investments.

## Violations Under Section 2(ii)(B) - Suitability to Customer's Profile

### 1. High-Risk Investments for Conservative Risk Profile

The customer is characterized as:
- 75 years old
- Retired aircraft maintenance worker
- Limited investment experience (2 years)
- Conservative risk tolerance
- Primary financial goal is retirement savings

However, the portfolio allocation shows:
- 70% of the portfolio is allocated to moderate-to-high or high-risk investments:
  - Growth Stock Fund (30%) - Moderate to High risk
  - Technology Sector ETF (25%) - High risk
  - Hedge Fund (15%) - High risk

FINRA specifically identifies "recommendation of high risk or alternative investments to a customer with a conservative risk tolerance" as a violation of Reg BI Care Obligation.

### 2. Excessive High-Risk Allocation

The "High Risk Investment Ratio" is 65% of the portfolio, which far exceeds what FINRA considers appropriate for a customer with moderate risk tolerance (where violations have been found at just 20%). For a customer with conservative risk tolerance, this allocation is even more inappropriate.

The "HighRiskNetworthRatio" of 45.5% indicates that a significant portion of the customer's net worth is invested in high-risk products, which is inappropriate given the customer's age, retirement status, and conservative risk profile.

## Potential Violations Under Section 2(ii)(C) - Excessive Trading

While the Annualized Portfolio Turnover rate of 5.8 falls just below the explicit violation threshold of 6.0 mentioned by FINRA, it is extremely close to this boundary. For a 75-year-old retired customer with conservative risk tolerance and limited investment experience, this turnover rate appears unsuitably high.

The Cost-to-Equity ratio of 18.5% is approaching the 20% threshold that FINRA identifies as indicating excessive trading. Given the customer's profile, even this level of trading costs is concerning.

## Conclusion

The broker/dealer has violated Section 2(ii) of Regulation Best Interest by:

1. Recommending investments that are clearly misaligned with the customer's investment profile, particularly regarding risk tolerance, age, retirement status, and investment experience
2. Allocating an excessive proportion of the portfolio to high-risk investments for a customer with conservative risk tolerance
3. Implementing an investment strategy that resulted in significant losses ($15,000) to the customer
4. Creating a portfolio with a turnover rate and cost-to-equity ratio that are inappropriate for the customer's profile and nearly meet FINRA's explicit thresholds for violations

These recommendations did not serve the best interest of the customer and appear to have placed the financial interests of the broker-dealer ahead of the customer's interests.

# Analysis of Violations Under Section (2)(ii) of Regulation Best Interest

Based on the provided customer information, allocation, transactions, and the law, there are multiple violations of Section (2)(ii) of Regulation Best Interest, specifically concerning the Care Obligation.

## Violations of the Care Obligation

### 1. High-Risk Allocation for a Moderate Risk Tolerance Customer

The customer's risk tolerance is stated as "Moderate," yet the portfolio contains a disproportionately high allocation to high-risk investments:
- 35% in GWG L Bonds (high risk)
- 15% in REITs (high risk)
- 15% in Private Equity Fund (very high risk)

This totals 65% of the portfolio in high or very high-risk investments, which is severely disproportionate for a customer with moderate risk tolerance. FINRA specifically notes violations when high-risk investments become disproportionate (as low as 20%) for moderate risk tolerance customers.

### 2. Misalignment with Investment Purpose

The transactions indicate that investments like GWG L Bonds (high risk) were recommended for "preservation of capital, current income, and funding retirement." This represents a fundamental misalignment, as high-risk speculative investments are not appropriate vehicles for capital preservation, especially for a retired 60-year-old with low income.

### 3. Unsuitable Investments for Customer Profile

The customer's profile shows:
- Age: 60
- Employment: Retired
- Income: $45K/year (categorized as "Low")
- Financial Goals: Retirement

Recommending that such a customer allocate 35% of their portfolio to GWG L Bonds and 15% to Private Equity (very high risk) violates the requirement to make recommendations in the best interest of the particular retail customer based on their investment profile.

### 4. Concentrated Position in Alternative Investments

The 35% allocation to GWG L Bonds represents a concentrated position in a single high-risk alternative investment. This concentration is particularly problematic given:
- The customer is retired
- The customer has moderate risk tolerance
- The stated investment purpose includes "preservation of capital"

### 5. Potential Failure in Due Diligence

While specific information about the broker's due diligence is not provided, the recommendation of GWG L Bonds (which have had significant issues in recent years, including bankruptcy) suggests potential failures in due diligence. This may include:
- Failure to properly understand the risks associated with GWG L Bonds
- Potentially relying too heavily on issuer-provided information rather than conducting independent verification
- Failure to consider reasonably available alternative investments at lower cost that would better align with the customer's moderate risk tolerance and retirement goals

## Conclusion

The broker's recommendations violated the Care Obligation under Section (2)(ii) of Regulation Best Interest by:
1. Recommending a portfolio with 65% high-risk investments to a moderate risk tolerance customer
2. Recommending investments unsuitable for the customer's age, income, retirement status, and financial goals
3. Creating a significant concentration in a single high-risk investment (GWG L Bonds) for a retired person with capital preservation goals
4. Potentially failing to conduct proper due diligence on the recommended investments

These violations resulted in substantial financial harm to the customer, with documented losses of $125,000.

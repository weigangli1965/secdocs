# Regulation Best Interest Care Obligation Violations - Section (2)(ii)

Based on the provided information, there are several clear violations of the Care Obligation under Regulation Best Interest section (2)(ii):

## Violation 1: Unsuitable Recommendations Based on Age and Experience
The customer is a 14-year-old minor with only 1 year of investment experience. The broker has recommended a portfolio containing:
- 75% high-risk investments
- 15% in Cryptocurrency ETFs (high risk)
- 30% in Technology Sector ETFs (high risk)
- 5% in Commodities Futures (very high risk)

This allocation violates section (2)(ii)(B) by failing to consider the customer's investment profile, particularly their age and experience level. The recommendations are not in the best interest of this particular retail customer given their lack of financial sophistication and legal status as a minor.

## Violation 2: Inappropriate Risk Level for Customer Profile
The broker recommended an extremely high-risk portfolio for a 14-year-old whose stated financial goal is "Education Funding." This violates the care obligation which requires recommendations be in the best interest of the particular customer based on their investment profile. Education funding typically requires more moderate and conservative investments, especially when the investor is a minor.

## Violation 3: Excessive Risk Concentration
The overall risk allocation shows 75% of investments in high or very high-risk products, with only 5% in low-risk investments (Savings Account). This extreme weighting toward speculative and high-risk instruments violates the requirement to ensure recommendations are in the best interest of the customer based on their investment profile.

## Violation 4: No Evidence of Guardian Involvement
As the customer is a minor (14 years old), there is no indication that a legal representative/guardian is involved in the investment decisions as would be required for a minor. Brokers have a legal obligation to work with a minor's legal representative when providing investment recommendations.

## Violation 5: Turnover Rate Concerns
The customer's annualized portfolio turnover rate is 5.2, approaching the 6.0 threshold that FINRA considers indicative of excessive trading. Given the customer is a minor with no income, this level of trading activity raises significant concerns about whether the series of recommended transactions is really in the customer's best interest as required by section (2)(ii)(C).

## Violation 6: Transaction Costs and Fees
The broker has charged $3,200 in commissions and $1,500 in other transaction costs, totaling $4,700 in fees on a $111,741 portfolio for a minor with no income. These costs further support the conclusion that the recommendations put the broker's financial interests ahead of the customer's best interests.

These violations are particularly egregious given the customer's status as a minor with minimal investment experience, no income, and a stated investment goal of education funding, yet being placed in predominantly high-risk, speculative investments that are clearly unsuitable for their profile.

Based on my analysis of the customer information, allocation, and transactions data against the care obligation in § 240.15l-1(a)(2)(ii), I have identified potential violations:

## Violation of Care Obligation § 240.15l-1(a)(2)(ii)(B)

There appears to be a significant misalignment between the customer's risk tolerance and the recommended investment portfolio:

1. **Disproportionate High-Risk Investments**: The customer's risk tolerance is classified as "Moderate," yet approximately 80% of their portfolio is allocated to high-risk or moderate-to-high risk investments:
   - Technology Sector ETF (35%, high risk)
   - Growth Stock Fund (30%, moderate to high risk)
   - Cryptocurrency ETF (15%, high risk)

2. **FINRA Standards Violation**: FINRA explicitly states that recommending a disproportionate amount of high-risk investments to customers with moderate risk tolerance constitutes a violation. The guidance notes that even allocations as low as 20% can be considered disproportionate. In this case, the high-risk allocation is approximately 4 times that threshold.

3. **Limited Experience Ignored**: The customer has only 2 years of investment experience, yet the broker recommended complex and highly volatile investments like Cryptocurrency ETFs, which require sophisticated knowledge to understand their risks.

4. **Net Worth Consideration**: With a High Risk to Net Worth ratio of 30%, the customer has significant exposure relative to their total assets, which is inconsistent with their moderate risk profile.

The broker appears to have failed to adequately consider the customer's investment profile, particularly their moderate risk tolerance and limited experience, when recommending this portfolio allocation. This represents a failure to act in the best interest of the retail customer as required under the Care Obligation.

While the customer's Annualized Portfolio Turnover (5.2) and Cost To Equity Ratio (15.8%) are below the thresholds that would automatically trigger concerns about excessive trading under § 240.15l-1(a)(2)(ii)(C), the core issue is the recommendation of an investment strategy that does not align with the customer's stated risk tolerance and investment experience.

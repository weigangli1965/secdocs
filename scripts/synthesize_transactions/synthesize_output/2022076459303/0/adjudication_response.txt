# Regulation Best Interest (Reg BI) Violations Under Section (2)(ii) Care Obligation

Based on the provided customer information, investment allocations, and transaction details, there are several significant violations of Regulation Best Interest under section (2)(ii), the Care Obligation:

## Violation 1: Inappropriate Investments for a Minor

The customer is a 13-year-old minor with only 1 year of investment experience. The broker has recommended a portfolio that is overwhelmingly concentrated in high and very high-risk investments:
- 35% in Technology Sector ETF (high risk)
- 25% in Growth Stock Fund (moderate to high risk)
- 15% in Cryptocurrency ETF (high risk)
- 10% in Hedge Fund (high risk)
- 8% in Private Equity Fund (very high risk)

In total, 93% of the portfolio is allocated to moderate-to-high, high, or very high-risk investments. This violates section (2)(ii)(B) of the Care Obligation, which requires brokers to have a reasonable basis to believe recommendations are in the best interest of the particular retail customer based on their investment profile.

## Violation 2: Disregard for Customer's Age and Limited Experience

The broker failed to properly consider the customer's extremely young age (13) and limited investment experience (1 year) when recommending complex and high-risk investments such as:
- Cryptocurrency ETFs
- Hedge Funds
- Private Equity Funds

These investments require sophisticated understanding of financial markets that would be beyond the reasonable capability of a 13-year-old investor, regardless of their stated risk tolerance.

## Violation 3: Inappropriate Risk Concentration

The High Risk Investment Ratio is 75% and the HighRiskNetworthRatio is 90%, which is excessively concentrated in risky assets for a minor. While the customer's risk tolerance is listed as "Aggressive," the broker still has a duty to ensure recommendations are appropriate given the customer's entire investment profile, including age and experience.

## Violation 4: Excessive Trading Concerns

The annualized portfolio turnover rate is 5.8, which is close to the 6.0 threshold that FINRA generally considers excessive. Combined with a cost-to-equity ratio of 18.5% (approaching the 20% threshold for potential violations), this suggests the account may be managed in a way that generates fees at the expense of appropriate investment strategy for a minor.

## Violation 5: Failure to Consider Educational Purpose

Despite the customer's stated financial goal of "Education Funding," the portfolio is constructed with extremely speculative investments that are inconsistent with typical education funding strategies, which generally prioritize capital preservation and moderate growth, especially for someone who may need these funds within a few years.

## Conclusion

The broker has clearly violated the Care Obligation under Reg BI section (2)(ii) by recommending investments that are inappropriate for a 13-year-old's age, experience level, and stated financial goals. The broker failed to reasonably consider the customer's investment profile when making these high-risk recommendations and created a portfolio that appears designed to generate fees rather than serve the best interests of this minor customer.

# Analysis of Potential Violations Under § 240.15l-1(a)(2)(ii)

Based on the provided information, there are potential violations of the Care Obligation under section (2)(ii) of Regulation Best Interest (Reg BI).

## Potential Violations

### 1. Due Diligence Issues (§ 240.15l-1(a)(2)(ii)(A))

The broker appears to have recommended private placement offerings by "Issuer 3" for pre-IPO shares of "Company B" without adequate evidence of due diligence. The transactions show significant investments ($150,000 and $100,000) in a high-risk, speculative pre-IPO offering without any indication that proper verification was performed about:

- Whether Issuer 3 actually possessed rights to the pre-IPO shares
- The markups being applied by Issuer 3
- The background and regulatory history of the Issuer's management team
- Independent verification of issuer-provided information

These are precisely the types of due diligence failures highlighted in the SEC guidance notes (points 1, 3, 7, and 10).

### 2. Suitability Issues (§ 240.15l-1(a)(2)(ii)(B))

Although the customer has an "Aggressive" risk tolerance and "Wealth Growth" as a financial goal, there are concerning factors:

- The customer experienced a $45,000 loss according to the customer profile
- The high-risk investments represent 60% of the portfolio (High Risk Investment Ratio)
- The customer's high-risk investments represent 25% of their net worth (HighRiskNetworthRatio)

The transactions show that between the first two purchases, the customer committed 41.7% of their total portfolio (25% + 16.7%) to a single high-risk, speculative pre-IPO investment through the same issuer. This concentration in one high-risk investment exceeds the 20% threshold mentioned in FINRA guidance as potentially disproportionate for customers with moderate risk tolerance.

### 3. Excessive Fees and Costs

The transactions reveal:
- $7,500 in fees/commissions on the first transaction (5% of $150,000)
- $5,000 in fees/commissions on the second transaction (5% of $100,000)
- Additional transaction costs of $2,000 and $1,500

These fees appear high, especially for a concentrated position in a single pre-IPO offering, and may indicate the broker placed their financial interests ahead of the customer's interests. The total fees and commissions ($12,500) plus other transaction costs ($3,500) amount to significant costs that would impact the customer's returns.

## Conclusion

The evidence suggests likely violations of the Care Obligation under section (2)(ii) of Reg BI. The broker appears to have:

1. Failed to conduct sufficient due diligence on the private placement offering
2. Recommended a highly concentrated position in a single high-risk investment
3. Charged significant fees and commissions without evidence that lower-cost alternatives were considered

These actions suggest the broker may have prioritized their own financial interests over the customer's best interest, particularly given the customer ultimately experienced financial loss despite their aggressive risk tolerance.

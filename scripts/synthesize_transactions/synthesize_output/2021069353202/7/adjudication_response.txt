## Potential Violations:

Based on the data provided, there appear to be potential violations of the Care Obligation under section (2)(ii)(B), which requires a broker to "have a reasonable basis to believe that the recommendation is in the best interest of a particular retail customer based on that retail customer's investment profile."

The primary concerns are:

1. **Excessive High-Risk Allocation:** The total allocation to high and very high-risk investments (approximately 25% of the portfolio) appears excessive for a customer with moderate risk tolerance whose primary financial goal is retirement. FINRA guidance specifically notes that allocations to high-risk investments as low as 20% can be disproportionate for moderate risk customers.

2. **Potentially Unsuitable Recommendations:** Some specific investments, particularly the Cryptocurrency ETF and Private Equity Fund, which are classified as high and very high risk respectively, may not align with the customer's moderate risk profile and retirement goals. These investments were made for "Speculative" purposes, which raises questions about their suitability for a retirement-focused portfolio.

This means the investments need to appreciate by 18.5% just to break even, which could be challenging to achieve consistently.

## Conclusion:

There appear to be potential violations of the Care Obligation under section (2)(ii) of Regulation Best Interest. The broker may have failed to properly align the investment recommendations with the customer's investment profile by recommending a disproportionate amount of high-risk investments for a customer with moderate risk tolerance and retirement objectives. The relatively high cost-to-equity ratio and turnover rate, while not technically exceeding FINRA's thresholds, further suggest that the recommendations may not have been in the best interest of the customer.

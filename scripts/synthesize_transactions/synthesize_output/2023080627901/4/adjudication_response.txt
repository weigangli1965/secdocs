## Violations Identified:

### 1. Excessive Trading (Violation of section 2(ii)(C))

The customer data shows an "Annualized Portfolio Turnover" rate of 5.8, which approaches the FINRA threshold of 6 that would generally indicate excessive trading. The transaction history shows a pattern of frequent buying and selling of the same securities within short time periods (often within 2-3 weeks), constituting "in-and-out trading." Examples include:

- Technology Sector ETF bought on February 15 and sold on March 2 (15 days)
- Growth Stock Fund bought on April 10 and sold on April 28 (18 days)
- Hedge Fund bought on June 12 and sold on July 5 (23 days)
- REIT bought on August 18 and sold on September 1 (14 days)
- Cryptocurrency ETF bought on October 15 and sold on October 29 (14 days)
- Technology Sector ETF bought on December 5 and sold on December 21 (16 days)

This pattern of frequent in-and-out trading appears designed primarily to generate commissions rather than serve the customer's stated investment objectives.

### 2. Risk Profile Mismatch (Violation of section 2(ii)(B))

The customer's risk tolerance is listed as "Moderate," yet the portfolio allocation shows that 45% of investments are in high-risk or very high-risk products:
- Technology Sector ETF (18%, High Risk)
- Real Estate Investment Trust (12%, High Risk)
- Hedge Fund (10%, High Risk)
- Cryptocurrency ETF (5%, High Risk)

FINRA guidance specifically states that a violation occurs when a broker recommends high-risk investments that become disproportionate (even as low as 20%) for a customer with moderate risk tolerance. At 45%, this far exceeds that threshold.

### 3. High Fees and Costs (Violation of section 2(ii)(B))

The Cost-to-Equity Ratio of 18.5% is approaching FINRA's 20% threshold that indicates excessive costs. The customer has paid $12,500 in fees and commissions plus $3,500 in other transaction costs, totaling $16,000 in a single year. This represents approximately 2.13% of the total portfolio value ($750K), which is excessive for the types of investments being recommended and traded.

### 4. Recommendation Pattern Contrary to Customer's Best Interest (Violation of section 2(ii)(A) and (B))

The broker appears to have failed to recommend a strategy that aligns with the customer's best interests. Instead of implementing a strategy consistent with the customer's moderate risk tolerance and wealth growth objectives, the broker engaged in a pattern of frequent trading in high-risk investments that generated substantial fees while resulting in significant losses ($75,000) for the customer.

In summary, the broker has violated the Care Obligation under Regulation Best Interest by engaging in excessive trading, recommending investments inconsistent with the customer's risk profile, generating excessive fees, and implementing a strategy that prioritized the broker's interests (through commission generation) over the customer's financial interests.

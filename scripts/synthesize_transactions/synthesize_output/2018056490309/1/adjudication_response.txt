## Potential Violations Under Care Obligation §240.15l-1(a)(2)(ii)(B)

1. **High-Risk Allocation for a Retired Customer**: While the customer's stated risk tolerance is "Aggressive," the overall allocation to high-risk investments (approximately 65% in combined high and very high-risk products) appears excessive for a 68-year-old retiree with an IRA account. Specifically:
   - 20% Technology Sector ETF (High Risk)
   - 15% Hedge Fund (High Risk)
   - 10% Cryptocurrency ETF (High Risk)
   - 7% REIT (High Risk)
   - 13% Moderate to High Risk (portion of Growth Stock Fund)

2. **Retirement Account Considerations**: The recommendations involve placing substantial high-risk investments in an IRA account, which is traditionally intended for retirement security. For a 68-year-old retiree, such aggressive allocations may not adequately protect retirement assets during market downturns, when recovery time is limited.

3. **Disproportionate High-Risk Investment Ratio**: The customer's portfolio has a "High Risk Investment Ratio" of 65%, which exceeds thresholds that FINRA has previously found problematic (as low as 20% for moderate risk tolerance customers). Even accounting for the stated "Aggressive" risk tolerance, this allocation appears excessive given the customer's retirement status and age.

4. **Failure to Consider the Complete Investment Profile**: The recommendations do not appear to adequately balance the customer's age (68) and employment status (retired) with the concentration of high-risk investments. While "Aggressive Growth" is listed as the investment purpose, this objective should be considered alongside other aspects of the customer's investment profile.

3. The customer does have stated "Aggressive" risk tolerance and "Wealth Growth" as a financial goal, which aligns somewhat with the investment strategies pursued.

## Conclusion

Based on SEC and FINRA interpretations of the Care Obligation under Regulation Best Interest, there appear to be violations related to the broker-dealer's obligation to have a reasonable basis to believe that recommendations are in the best interest of this particular retail customer based on their complete investment profile. The high concentration of high-risk investments (65%) for a 68-year-old retiree using an IRA account raises significant concerns about whether these recommendations appropriately balanced the customer's age, retirement status, and need for some degree of capital preservation, despite their stated aggressive risk tolerance.

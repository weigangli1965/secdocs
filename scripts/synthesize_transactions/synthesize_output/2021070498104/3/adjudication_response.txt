# Analysis of Violations under §240.15l-1(a)(2)(ii) Care Obligation

Based on the provided information, there appear to be multiple violations of the Care Obligation under section (2)(ii) as interpreted by the SEC and FINRA.

## Violation 1: Unsuitable Recommendation Based on Customer's Investment Profile

The customer has a "Moderate" risk tolerance as explicitly stated in their profile. However, the broker recommended "GWG L Bonds" which are classified as "high" risk investments. The transactions show that a total of 45% of the customer's portfolio (from multiple purchases) was allocated to these high-risk bonds, which is significantly disproportionate for a customer with moderate risk tolerance.

FINRA guidelines specifically note violations occur when recommending "high risk or alternative investments to a customer with a conservative risk tolerance" or when "high-risk investments to customers with a moderate risk tolerance" become "disproportionate (as low as 20% in some cases)." With 45% of the portfolio in high-risk bonds, this clearly exceeds the disproportionate threshold for a moderate risk investor.

## Violation 2: Age-Inappropriate Investment

The customer is 68 years old and retired, with their financial goal listed as "Retirement." High-risk investments like GWG L Bonds are generally not suitable for retirement-focused senior investors who typically need more stability and income preservation. The broker failed to properly consider the customer's age, retirement status, and investment time horizon when recommending such a significant allocation to high-risk bonds.

## Violation 3: Failure to Consider Alternative Investments

The broker appears to have concentrated recommendations in a single high-risk product (GWG L Bonds) rather than considering reasonably available alternatives at lower risk and cost that would better align with the customer's moderate risk profile and retirement needs. This violates the requirement to "Have a reasonable basis to believe that the recommendation is in the best interest of a particular retail customer."

## Violation 4: Potential Lack of Due Diligence

While not explicitly stated in the provided information, the fact that the customer lost $125,000 (as indicated in their profile) and that they had to sell portions of their GWG L Bonds investments suggests there may have been issues with the investment that proper due diligence would have revealed. The broker has an obligation to "understand the potential risks, rewards, and costs associated with the recommendation" which may not have been adequately fulfilled.

## Conclusion

The broker violated the Care Obligation under section (2)(ii) by recommending high-risk GWG L Bonds that were unsuitable for the customer's moderate risk tolerance, retirement status, age, and financial goals. The disproportionately high allocation to these bonds (45%) exceeds FINRA's threshold for what would be considered appropriate for a moderate risk investor, and the transactions resulted in significant financial losses for the customer.

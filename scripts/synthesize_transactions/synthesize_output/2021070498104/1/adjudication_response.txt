# Analysis of Regulation Best Interest Violations - Section 2(ii) Care Obligation

After reviewing the customer information, portfolio allocation, transaction history, and relevant law, I've identified potential violations of the Care Obligation under Regulation Best Interest Section 2(ii).

## Violations Found:

### 1. Risk Profile Mismatch - Section 2(ii)(B)
- Customer has a "Moderate" risk tolerance, but the portfolio contains:
  - 45% allocation to high-risk investments
  - 20% to "very high" risk Private Equity Funds
  - 5% to GWG L Bonds (high risk)
  - Additional high-risk investments in Technology ETFs (10%), REITs (10%), and Hedge Funds (5%)
  
  This disproportionate high-risk allocation (45%) substantially exceeds what FINRA considers appropriate for a moderate risk customer (where even 20% can be considered excessive).

### 2. GWG L Bonds Due Diligence Issues - Section 2(ii)(A)
- The recommendation of GWG L Bonds raises concerns about adequate due diligence. GWG L Bonds are complex, high-risk products that likely required enhanced scrutiny regarding:
  - The legitimacy of the issuer's business model
  - Verification of the issuer's claims beyond issuer-provided materials
  - Proper consideration of alternatives with lower risk profiles

### 3. Fee Structure Concerns - Section 2(ii)(B)
- The disproportionately high fees for certain transactions suggest potential violations:
  - GWG L Bonds: $7,500 commission on a $100,000 investment (7.5% commission rate)
  - This suggests the broker may have placed their financial interests ahead of the customer's interests by recommending high-commission products over more suitable alternatives

### 4. Overall Suitability Issues - Section 2(ii)(B)
- The 55-year-old customer has experienced significant losses ($350,000) in their portfolio
- Given the customer's age and moderate risk tolerance, the portfolio appears unsuitably aggressive
- The broker failed to adequately consider the customer's investment profile when making these high-risk recommendations

While the portfolio's turnover rate (4.5) and cost-to-equity ratio (18.5%) are below FINRA's excessive trading thresholds (6 and 20% respectively), the other elements described above strongly indicate violations of the Care Obligation under Regulation Best Interest.

Based on the provided information, I will analyze whether violations occurred under section (2)(ii) of the care obligation in Regulation Best Interest.

There appears to be a potential violation of the care obligation under section (2)(ii)(B), which requires brokers to have a reasonable basis to believe their recommendations are in the best interest of the particular retail customer based on their investment profile.

The reasons for the potential violation are:

1. **Disproportionate High-Risk Investments**: While the customer has an "Aggressive" risk tolerance, their portfolio shows a "High Risk Investment Ratio" of 60%. FINRA guidance indicates that high-risk investments becoming disproportionate in a portfolio (as low as 20% in some cases) can constitute a violation, even for customers with moderate risk tolerance. With 60% of the portfolio in high-risk investments, this appears excessive even for an aggressive investor.

2. **Leveraged and Inverse ETFs Recommendations**: The transactions show multiple recommendations for leveraged and inverse exchange-traded funds (NT-ETFs), which are classified as high-risk products. These complex products often carry significant risks that may not be appropriate for retail investors, regardless of risk tolerance. 3. **Customer Loss**: The customer profile shows an "Amount Lost" of $45,000, which suggests these recommendations led to poor outcomes. FINRA specifically finds violations when recommendations lead to losses for customers.

In conclusion, there appears to be a violation of the Care Obligation under section (2)(ii)(B) due to the recommendation of disproportionately high-risk investments that resulted in significant losses for the customer, potentially placing the broker's financial interests ahead of the customer's interests.
